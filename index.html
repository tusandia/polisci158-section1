About Me

I am a fourth-year History major completing an interdisciplinary honors thesis with the Feminist, Gender, and Sexuality Studies department. I am interested in how laws change over time, and in who those laws work for and who they leave out.

I am pre-law, with a particular interest in child advocacy and family law.

Research Interest

My research looks at access to justice for domestic violence survivors in South Asian immigrant communities in California. I study this as a legal history, following how domestic violence law and immigration law have developed together since the 1990s.

I am especially interested in:

  • Federal protections for survivors, including the Violence Against Women Act and its reauthorizations
  • How California courts treat religious marriage contracts as evidence
  • How survivors and advocacy organizations have shaped the law through their own work

For this class, I am interested in creating a law index of domestic violence law in California and federal law, so that the statutes and protections I study can be found and compared in one place.

Project Idea

This project is my honors thesis.

The Burden of Proof: Access to Justice for Female Domestic Violence Survivors in South Asian Immigrant Communities in California

This project is a legal history of domestic violence and immigration law in California from 1994 to the present, told through the experiences and self-advocacy of South Asian women. It follows two threads side by side: federal protections for survivors, and California case law on religious marriage contracts. It asks where the legal system has moved toward survivors' real circumstances, and where it has not.

Planned structure

  • 1994–2000: The passage of the Violence Against Women Act and early advocacy
  • 2000–2013: Court cases on marriage contracts and the reauthorization era
  • 2013–present: The post-9/11 climate and ongoing gaps in the law
